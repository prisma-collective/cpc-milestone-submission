## Executive Summary

This report presents the findings of Phase 2 (Regional Screening and Desk Research) of the PRISMA research programme on government and emerging-market adoption playbooks for Cardano. Building on the research design, evaluation rubrics, and stakeholder interview guides established in [Milestone 1](../milestone-1/index.md), the research team evaluated the eight candidate regional clusters across Latin America, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Southern Europe using enacted legislation, central bank circulars, multilateral payment series, on-chain liquidity telemetry, and documented local platform benchmarks as of November 2026.

Phase 2 narrows the eight candidate clusters down to the three priority emerging-market regions that warrant intensive primary fieldwork in Phase 3, sets out falsifiable use-case hypotheses for each market, and maps the target institutions required to test them.

### Key Regional Screening Findings

1. **Three Priority Emerging-Market Regions Advance to Phase 3 Primary Fieldwork (`3 Regional Hubs / 9 Local Teams / 42-51 Target Interviews`):**
   - **Southern Cone (`Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay` - Weighted RARF Score: `3.40 / 5.00` | `68.0 / 100`):** Anchored by the **Buenos Aires Hub** (`3 Local Teams`), the Southern Cone ranks first overall. Persistent domestic inflation and foreign-exchange settlement friction in Argentina drive Latin America's highest stablecoin usage (over 60% of national crypto transaction volume). Recent legal reforms, including Argentina's Executive Decree `DNU 70/2023` (permitting private contracts to be denominated and settled in virtual assets), the National Securities Commission's `General Resolution 1058/2025` Virtual Asset Service Provider (`PSAV`) registry, and Uruguay's Virtual Assets Law `20.345`, establish a workable legal framework for business-to-business cooperative dollar escrow and cross-border Mercosur contractor settlement. Phase 3 fieldwork will conduct `14-17` primary interviews testing these two workflows alongside Buenos Aires City's `QuarkID` digital identity network.
   - **West Africa (`Ghana, Nigeria, Senegal` - Weighted RARF Score: `3.25 / 5.00` | `65.0 / 100`):** Anchored by the **Lagos Hub** (`3 Local Teams` covering Lagos, Abuja, Northern Nigerian university networks, and Accra), West Africa ranks second. High formal remittance costs (`7.9%-8.4%`) and manual credential verification coincide with new statutory frameworks in both Nigeria (`Investments and Securities Act 2025`, the Securities and Exchange Commission's `ARIP` licensing cohort, the Central Bank of Nigeria's December 2023 banking circular, and the `Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023`) and Ghana (`Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, December 2025 [Act 1154]`). Phase 3 fieldwork will conduct `15-18` interviews examining wholesale cross-border payment corridors, multi-institution academic and trade guild credentials, and a short 1-to-2 call diagnostic on state land-registry constraints.
   - **East Africa (`Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda` - Weighted RARF Score: `3.20 / 5.00` | `64.0 / 100`):** Anchored by the **Nairobi Hub** (`3 Local Teams` covering Nairobi, Kigali, and Kampala), East Africa ranks third among emerging markets. Two regulatory developments define the region's near-term readiness: the entry into force of Kenya's `Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025` on `4 November 2025` (paired with the `Finance Act 2025` replacement of the `3%` gross digital asset tax with a `10%` excise duty on service fees) and the European Union's Deforestation Regulation (`EUDR`, Regulation `2023/1115`), which requires coffee, tea, and cocoa exporters to provide verifiable plot-level geolocation and custody-chain records. Phase 3 fieldwork will conduct `13-16` interviews focused on agricultural export traceability and cross-border East African Community and humanitarian payment corridors.
2. **Four Candidate Clusters Retained on Desk Monitoring (`0 Active Phase 3 Hubs / 0 Phase 3 Interviews`):**
   - **Iberia (`Spain - Barcelona` - Weighted RARF Score: `3.20 / 5.00` | `64.0 / 100`):** Spain provides a clear European statutory baseline (`EU MiCA`, `eIDAS 2.0`, `Law 9/2017 Article 118` minor contracts under `EUR 15,000`, and `Royal Decree-Law 14/2019` off-chain privacy rules). Because domestic retail payments in Spain are already well served by `SEPA Instant` and `Bizum` and this engagement funds three emerging-market fieldwork hubs, Iberia remains in the desk monitoring tier as a European reference benchmark alongside Brazil, the Andean countries, and Mexico.
   - **Brazil (`3.05`), Andean (`2.80`), and Mexico (`2.75`):** In **Brazil**, `BCB Pix` (`over 175M` users) handles domestic retail payments at negligible cost, while Central Bank Resolutions `519, 520, and 521/2025` (effective February 2026) regulate cross-border stablecoin transfers as foreign exchange and require `BRL 2M-14M` in minimum capital from licensed providers. In the **Andean** cluster (`Colombia, Peru`), Peru's `Yape` and `Plin` and Colombia's `Nequi` and `Bre-B` cover everyday retail transfers while Colombia still lacks an enacted virtual-asset licensing statute following the conclusion of the `LaArenera` sandbox. In **Mexico**, `Bitso` already settles over `USD 8B` annually (`~10%+` of the US-Mexico remittance corridor) directly into `Banxico SPEI`, while `Banxico Circular 4/2019` bars regulated fintechs and banks from client-facing virtual-asset operations. With no funded fieldwork hubs in these countries under this contract, all three remain on desk monitoring.
3. **One Candidate Cluster Excluded from Near-Term Entry (`No-Go`):**
   - **Horn / Ethiopia (`2.05 / 5.00` - `No-Go`):** Despite severe foreign-exchange scarcity, the National Bank of Ethiopia's May 2022 public notice and `Proclamation No. 1282/2023` prohibit cryptocurrency settlement, and the federal government has consolidated national digital identity and school records onto **`Fayda`**, built on the non-blockchain **`MOSIP`** platform (`Proclamation No. 1284/2023`, `over 13M-36M` enrolled under a `USD 350M` World Bank programme).
